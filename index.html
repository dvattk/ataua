Frontier models are not Pareto optimal

No model reaches the Pareto frontier. For every model, an allocation exists that raises provider reward without lowering user reward.

Enough seats for every user

Abundant supply

Frontier models are not Pareto optimal

When supply is abundant, no model reaches the Pareto frontier either. Every model's outcome could be improved for providers at no cost to users.

Why the gap?

Individual decisions can undermine collective outcomes

Agent A booked the Delta seats, so Agent B settled for a pricier United trip. A coordinated allocation raises user reward from 0.88 to 0.93 and provider reward from 0.40 to 0.93.

Agents are good buyers but poor sellers

Provider agents could hold the fare, offer another cabin or add paid services. Instead, every booking received the full 30% discount, while customers spent only about half their budgets.

Does a pricier provider agent help?

4.1×

A higher provider inference cost does not consistently bring higher reward for either side. Lower-cost provider agents can match or beat more expensive ones.
